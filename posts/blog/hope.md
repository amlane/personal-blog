I've been living in a place between hope and attachment for a very large part of my adult life. 
<br />
<br />
It feels like I have been wandering a desert searching for water without a map or strategy, not realizing that hope itself was never a guarantee I would ever find it. While that hope has been a helpful emotion, acting as the encouragement I needed to keep taking steps forward when confronted with uncertainty and adversity, it has also allowed me to form attachments to specific outcomes leading to destructive decisions. 
<br />
<br />
Hope is the belief that if I keep moving, it is possible I will find water.</br>
Attachment is the idea that it can only come from a clear blue lake that is ice-cold.
<br />
<br />
For as long as I remember, I have carried the hope that I would meet a consistent person who wanted to give me the love and protection I have not yet experienced in my life. I became attached to the idea that the only way I would feel this was by finding a romantic partner to build a life with. This attachment has led to me making choices that were not in my best interest. 
<br />
<br />
In my 20s, I would date anyone who showed me the least bit of interest, even if I knew that the relationship wasn't heading in a good direction or bringing out the best in me. 
In my 30s, I started to abstain from dating altogether. I would spend months/years waiting on a specific person to reciprocate feelings hoping that I could prove I was worthy of their love. 
Two ends of the spectrum. Both with the same desired outcome. Both left me feeling like I invested time and effort that didn't lead to the future I had hoped for.
<br />
<br />
I can become so attached to an ideal outcome, that I would walk right past a cactus, completely dismissing the opportunity. While the cactus may not be what I had expected, it contains the very thing I have been hoping for. 
<br />
<br />
On the flip side, when I was laid off from my Sales Management job in 2018, I took a chance and made a career pivot. I focused all my energy on myself while attending a coding bootcamp. I had hope that I would become a software engineer but there was no certainty that I would ever get a role in such a competitive industry. After months of dedication to building and mentoring others through the same bootcamp program, I was able to land my first developer job where I worked my way from a Technical Implementation Consultant to Chief Engineer of two teams of developers. 
<br />
<br />
Hope has been both an effective tool and a destructive force in my life. Upon reflection, I realize now it is when I grip tightly to an attachment of a particular outcome that my judgment becomes impaired. I start ignoring my intuition and any evidence that doesn't confirm my bias.
<br />
<br />
All this is to say, I hope I never lose the courage to hope - even with the knowledge of how it has misled me (and has the potential to do again). I choose to believe these detours are what transform me. Each unexpected turn shaping me into a more compassionate, resilient and better human. I can only hope.
