My kitchen floor was in need of a deep clean. Even with enough disposable income to hire a maid, I still prefer to do my own housekeeping. During deeply transformative periods in my life, I find myself with an undeniable urge to rearrange, declutter, and disinfect. Cleaning has become a spiritual practice for me; a sort of meditative experience leading me to creative ideas or solutions to problems I hadn't considered. 
<br />
<br />
I filled up a bucket with warm soapy water and grabbed a sponge, ready to put in that old fashioned elbow grease. As I made my way across the faux wood linoleum, my awareness shifted back to the bucket, now filled with a cloudy, murky solution. Regardless I carried on, dunking the sponge and wiping down surfaces. As I scrubbed away a splotch from the wall next to my fridge, a grayish brown glaze was now layered over the eggshell white walls. 
<br />
<br />
I let out a sigh. Stubbornly admitting my defeat, I stood up, grabbed my bucket and went to the sink. As I stood there waiting, I entertained the thought that the dirty water was a symbol of my current mental state. I had been moving through life, going from one task to the next without ever taking the time to replenish my water. The compounding mess on the wall was a symbol of the work I had been producing. Due to this perpetual state of being, I was no longer able to approach life with a clear vision or a fresh perspective. Everything was becoming covered with a residue of resentment and pure exhaustion.
<br />
<br />
Returning to the floor with my fresh bucket, I was able to finish my chore. Standing up, I took in a deep breath and appreciated the sweet smell of lavender Fabuloso dancing on my olfactory system, admiring a job well done.   
<br />
The word "rest" conjures certain images in my mind. Sometimes it is me sunbathing on a beach at an all-inclusive resort in some far away land. Other times it is simply me in my favorite sweatpants lounging around my house for a week ordering takeout. I've turned the idea of rest into an escape fantasy rather than learning how to integrate it into my actual reality. 
<br />
<br />
Growing up in a blue collar family in the Midwest, I learned the value of hard work and many of my experiences taught me that my level of output is directly proportional to my value as a person. It was only once I started writing this reflection that I began to understand the origin of my resistance to rest.  
<br />
Over the years, I have witnessed my mom go through a cycle of moving her way up to achieve major success, only to end up in a life-altering burnout. And as generational cycles go, I've allowed the same pattern to occur in my own life.
<br />
<br />
I have experienced this cycle at least twice in my life. Both resulted in bed-rotting for a week watching terrible reruns on TV, ruminating on events I couldn't change and wallowing in self-pity. 
<br />
<br />
These experiences shaped my distorted views on rest. I had come to believe that taking time for it would only lead to feelings of shame and regret. I knew logically it is necessary but because I didn't know how to rest effectively, I kept finding myself in the same cycle of only prioritizing it when I hit my absolute limit. However, if there's one thing I have learned, it is that if you don't carve out time for rest, life has a way of forcing it on you. 
<br />
<br />
After my kitchen adventure, I went back to my routine, forgetting all about the dirty mop bucket. I didn't have time for metaphors and reflections. I had Slack notifications, Google calendar events and Jira tickets that needed my time and attention.
<br />
<br />
Time passed and a couple months later, on a regular Wednesday afternoon, I was let go from my job under very unexpected circumstances. 
<br />
<br />
I was disoriented the first couple of days, not really knowing what to do with myself but also gripped with the fear that I would end up wasting another week of my life in bed. I jumped into action. I started networking, updating my resume, applying for jobs and keeping a healthy amount of tasks on my to do list. Within the first few days of being unemployed, I had multiple leads on jobs and interviews lined up. I was determined to stay busy.
<br />
<br />
One task I gave myself was completing a list of goals using the 5/25 rule. This is a goal-setting strategy where you write out 25 goals and circle the five most important ones to prioritize and regard the rest as a distraction. The perfect activity to fill my time.
<br />
<br />
I sat down ready to bust out my list of goals... but I struggled to even come up with five, let alone twenty-five. This wasn't the first time I experienced avolition so I knew it is an alarm bell. A warning that I am approaching burnout. Still grappling with the fear of rest turning into a triathlon of takeout, depression hair and binge watching Netflix I wasn't sure what to do about it. 
<br />
<br />
Amidst all this, an old friend reached out asking to stay at my place for the weekend. The same friend who showed up at my door the first time I laid in bed for a week after the end of a tumultuous seven year long relationship. I remembered how she coaxed me off the futon. Driving us three hours to the Berry Patch to spend the day foraging and enjoying blueberry lemonade. Coming home with a giant bag of blueberries, I baked the best cobbler in the history of fruit pastries. That day left me feeling more refreshed than an entire week in bed. 
That memory led me to start asking myself...
<br />
<br />
What leaves me feeling inspired? 
What brings back my spark?
What allows me to show up eager to take on whatever comes my way?
<br />
<br />
I discovered it is having meaningful conversations with people. 
Getting in movement, like hiking or bicycling, especially in nature. 
Consuming content that is uplifting or educational.
Making something just for the pure enjoyment of it (like my blueberry cobbler).
<br />
<br />
Equipped with this new awareness about what truly refuels me, I felt safe to give myself permission to rest. After my friend left I decided to take a day where I had no particular goal. No to do list or solid plans. I didn't need to commit to making it a week long event. Just a day. I ended up going for a walk on the trail near my house. Nothing extraordinary. Just getting in my steps and enjoying the sunshine. 
<br />
<br />
About halfway through the walk, without even trying, a flood of ideas came to me. Some of them old ideas, long forgotten to a thief we call time. Some were new ideas or solutions to little annoyances I have been living with. I quickly noted them down before they had a chance to slip away. I felt a smile returning to my face. _I had goals again._ A plan for how I wanted to spend my time that excited me. At least for the next 6 months. Which was enough for me. It was a start. A sign of life; like a little seedling sprouting from my soul's garden reminding me that all it takes is a little bit of fresh water to regrow what has been lost. 
<br />
<br />
With thoughts and ideas now flowing out of me, I opened up my laptop to the 5/25 list and began typing. Just a day ago, what felt so difficult and discouraging was now effortless and motivating. As I was listing out all the things I wanted to accomplish, more kept popping up. Then followed the steps I could take to achieve those goals along with reasons why those goals mattered to me in the first place. It was like I had tapped into some cosmic force that was revealing a part of myself that I didn't previously have access to. None my goals are particularly revolutionary. Some of them had even come up in my mind when I had first attempted the list, but now that inner critic I had allowed to dismiss and judge them, was no longer running the show. 
<br />
<br />
Rest isn't about stopping or standing still. It's about learning what restores you. Understanding which experiences allow you to return to yourself. You can't predict what outcomes you will get from it but what you can know for certain is that fresh water will produce a better harvest. 
<br />
<br />
And on that note, I am ready to put this reflection to bed.
